Veterinary testing on our existing analysers

Calibration, verification and validation plan for the ARCHITECT, MAGLUMI, Mindray 7-part CBC, BioSystems BA200 and the real-time PCR laboratory.

Prepared 15 October 2026 for the laboratory director and quality manager. Technical planning document; not legal advice.

1. Summary

None of our analysers needs a different calibration to accept animal samples: calibrators are traceable to human reference materials and stay on the manufacturers' schedules. What changes is verification and validation. Each test must be shown to work in each species' blood, on our method, and be reported against reference intervals set on that method. Some tests cannot be done on human platforms at all and need a veterinary reagent, a veterinary analyser or a partner laboratory.

2. Calibration, verification and validation

  • Calibration sets the instrument's response with the manufacturer's calibrators. Keep each system's calibration schedule unchanged (new reagent lot, interval in the instructions for use, after major maintenance). Do not recalibrate with animal material.
  • Verification shows that a veterinary-labelled method performs as its maker claims in our hands (BA200 veterinary reagents, veterinary PCR kits, a veterinary haematology analyser).
  • Validation shows that a method not designed for animals gives clinically usable results in a given species (human hormone immunoassays, the human Mindray analyser). ASVCP guidelines say this must be done before a new test or instrument is used.
Why human assays need species validation. In dogs, cats and horses, chemiluminescent total T4 read about 30-40% lower than radioimmunoassay, a bias not seen in human samples (Singh 1997). Immulite 2000 total T4 gave a slope of 0.76 in dogs and 0.71 in cats against a reference method (Wolff 2020). Canine progesterone by chemiluminescence read significantly higher than by radioimmunoassay three days before ovulation, which changes breeding decisions. Results are therefore method-specific: reference intervals and decision limits must come from our own method.

3. Instrument plans

3.1 BioSystems BA200: veterinary chemistry

The BA200 is made by BioSystems S.A. (Spain), not Applied Biosystems; please confirm the label on our unit. BioSystems markets the BA200 for veterinary analysis. Its veterinary reagent range covers more than 120 parameters, filterable by species (canine, feline, bovine, camel, equine, poultry, reptiles and exotics), and it lists veterinary reference materials (VetBC). The BA200 has 88 reagent and sample positions and an optional ISE for sodium, potassium and chloride.

  • Calibration: use the calibrator named in each veterinary reagent's instructions; same schedule as human tests.
  • Verification: precision and method comparison in canine and feline pools for each analyte; bovine pools for the dairy profile.
  • Quality control: veterinary control material at two levels on every run, plus a pooled in-house species serum.
  • Albumin: bromocresol green (BCG) reads higher than electrophoresis in dogs and horses, lower in ruminants and chickens, and higher still in canine heparin plasma because of fibrinogen. Use serum, report against method-specific species intervals.
  • Pancreatitis: Spec cPL is an IDEXX test. DGGR lipase correlated closely with it in 301 dogs (rs 0.91; kappa 0.87 at 143 U/L for cPL above 200 ug/L). Offer DGGR lipase and set our own cut-off.
  • Add to the menu: fructosamine, bile acids, CK, GGT, phosphorus, magnesium, beta-hydroxybutyrate (dairy ketosis), urine protein and creatinine for the UPC ratio.
  • Not possible: SDMA (the validated immunoassay is an IDEXX product). Offer creatinine and UPC, and send SDMA to a partner if needed.

3.2 Mindray 7-part differential analyser: haematology

Our analyser is a human model. No Mindray veterinary species mode for the human BC-6800, BC-6000 or BC-760 series was found.

Known limits of human analysers with animal blood: feline platelets overlap red cells in size, so most analysers cannot count them; platelet clumps (common in cats) give falsely low counts, which should be reported as minimums; birds, reptiles and fish have nucleated red cells and cannot be analysed automatically; some mammals have red cells small enough to be confused with platelets; the white-cell differential is gated for human cells.
  • If we use the human analyser: validate RBC, HGB, HCT, MCV and WBC for dogs by comparison with a veterinary analyser on at least 40 samples; perform a 100-cell blood-film differential on every animal CBC; estimate feline platelets from the film; review the film for parasites (Babesia, Ehrlichia morulae, Hepatozoon).
  • Recommended: add a veterinary analyser. Mindray's animal-health company sells the BC-60R Vet (6-part differential, 33 parameters, scatter-plus-fluorescence, about 40 samples an hour, feline-friendly, many species) and the BC-5000 Vet (5-part, 13 built-in species including dog, cat, horse, cow, goat and sheep). A veterinary analyser needs only verification, not full validation.
  • Sample timing: canine EDTA platelet counts begin to fall by 12 h and MCV rises within 12-24 h; ask clinics for a fresh smear with every EDTA tube.

3.3 Abbott ARCHITECT: hormones

We found no published validation of ARCHITECT hormone immunoassays (total T4, free T4, cortisol, progesterone, insulin) in dogs or cats. This is a negative search result, not proof that none exists; ask Abbott India for any veterinary data. On the chemistry side, a canine-specific CRP reagent (Gentian) has been validated on the ARCHITECT c4000 (Hillstrom 2014), which matters only if we have a c-series module.

3.4 Snibe MAGLUMI: hormones

Snibe lists no veterinary reagent line or veterinary analyser, and no peer-reviewed MAGLUMI validation in any animal species was found. Run the same T4, cortisol and progesterone validation on the MAGLUMI in parallel with the ARCHITECT, using the same samples. For each analyte, keep the platform with lower bias against the reference laboratory and better low-end precision as the veterinary method, and the other as a documented backup.

3.5 Real-time PCR laboratory

Avian influenza: do not test. Avian influenza is a scheduled disease under the Prevention and Control of Infectious and Contagious Diseases in Animals Act, 2009. ICAR-NIHSAD Bhopal is designated to confirm any case in India. Our H1N1 and H3N2 assays are human diagnostics and must not be used on animal samples. Refer suspected avian influenza to the State Animal Husbandry Department.
  • Instrument: an open real-time PCR system can run veterinary kits. Check each kit's validated instruments, dye channels and passive reference before purchase.
  • Kits found on the market: Retro Biotech (Jaipur) multiplex veterinary kits including Brucella; YouSeq canine distemper and parvovirus multiplex, plus Babesia and Ehrlichia assays; Ring Bio Flexy Pet canine babesiosis and parvovirus; Gold Standard Diagnostics INgene q Babesia; Thermo Fisher VetMAX MastiType for bovine mastitis (validated on ABI 7500 and QuantStudio 5). Indian researchers have published multiplex qPCRs for Babesia gibsoni, B. vogeli, Ehrlichia canis and Hepatozoon canis.
  • Verification per kit: limit of detection by dilution series, repeatability, positive and negative extraction controls on every run, and comparison with a reference laboratory on 20 or more positive and negative field samples per target.
  • Extraction: verify the extraction kit separately for EDTA blood, faeces, urine and milk.
  • Segregation: process animal samples in separate extraction batches with distinct labels and an LIS species flag, to prevent mix-ups and cross-contamination with human work.
  • Scheduled diseases: lumpy skin disease and other diseases in the Act's Schedule carry reporting duties. Agree a written procedure with the Animal Husbandry Department and refer positives for confirmation (Western Regional Disease Diagnostic Laboratory, Pune).
  • Biosafety: Brucella is internationally classed as risk group 3; do not culture it. Extract all animal samples in a Class II biosafety cabinet.

Start by transferring intervals for dogs and cats with 20 healthy animals per species and analyte (for example from Nagpur Veterinary College clinics and partner practices, with owner consent). Report each species, and adult and young animals where needed, separately. Livestock intervals for the dairy profile follow once a herd partner is on board.
